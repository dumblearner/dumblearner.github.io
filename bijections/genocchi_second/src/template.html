The second bijection

A D-permutation satisfies σ(i) ≥ i for odd i, and σ(i) ≤ i for even i. Read values i = 1, …, 2n. The parity of σ⁻¹(i) gives the path step: even means rise; odd means fall.

A free vertex has no permutation edge yet. Rank free vertices from left to right, starting at 0. The pair ξᵢ = (ξ′ᵢ, ξ″ᵢ) records the chosen top and bottom ranks. Where applicable, include the newly added vertex as the last candidate; choosing it gives a fixed point.

The path may touch −1. Replace every dip 0 → −1 → 0 by one horizontal step of length 2, labeled (0, 0). This is the labeled 0-Schröder path.

Solid arrows run from the top row to the bottom. Optional dotted arrows i′ → i join the two copies of each index, making cycles visible (Lemma 7.7). A peak closes a cycle exactly when its selected endpoints belong to the same open chain.

About this animation

Based on Bishal Deb and Alan D. Sokal, Classical continued fractions for some multivariate polynomials generalizing the Genocchi and median Genocchi numbers (2022), §7, especially §§7.1–7.3, Lemma 7.7, and Figure 11.

Three views, one construction. The bipartite view is Figure 11’s model. The Laguerre view identifies j with j′: each vertex has indegree and outdegree at most 1, as defined in Bishal Deb’s Laguerre digraph paper, §6.2. It retains this animation’s chronological step order, rather than the different edge-insertion order in that paper. Ranks always follow increasing vertex labels, even when components are rearranged.

The arc view follows Figure 1 and §2.8: increasing edges above the axis, decreasing edges below, fixed points without arcs, and arrowheads omitted. Counters use only present arcs and four distinct endpoints; joinings and pseudo-nestings are excluded.

Figure 11 correction. Its printed cycle decomposition does not match its one-line permutation. This animation uses the one-line permutation and diagram; its correct cycles agree with Figure 4.

Visual homage to the colored transparencies and progressive visual proofs of Xavier Viennot’s The Art of Bijective Combinatorics. Independently made; no affiliation. All diagrams are drawn from the computed bijection.

Random examples are generated from legal labeled paths; this is not uniform sampling of D-permutations.
